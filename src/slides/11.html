Guess first: at 45 °C instead of 15 °C, the take-off run gets...
Longer. Hot air is less dense, so each metre per second of speed makes less lift. Pilots call it a hot-and-high day.

Where you meet it

1
Kuwait summers reach 45 to 50 °C. Thin, hot air means less lift per metre of runway, so airlines limit weight or prefer cooler departure hours on the hottest days.
2
Flaps are the slats and panels you see sliding out of the wing before take-off: they raise CL, which is exactly the slider you moved.
3
Next flight, count the seconds from brakes-off to lift-off and look at the runway markers. Then compare with the simulation's number.
